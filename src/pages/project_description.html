The COLIBRE Project

Motivation

COLIBRE is the latest in the series of simulation projects that includes OWLS, Cosmo-OWLS, EAGLE, BAHAMAS, and FLAMINGO. Its objectives are most comparable to those of EAGLE, but it improves on that model in many ways. In terms of numerics, the main improvements include the suppression of the spurious transfer of energy by using four times more DM than baryonic particles, the use of 20 times more particles in total, the use of a new hydrodynamics code Swift, and a new structure finder. In terms of physics, all subgrid prescriptions are new or have been significantly modified. The most important improved or new features are that cooling below 104K is allowed (i.e.\ no equation of state is imposed on the ISM), the growth and composition of dust grains are simulated, self-shielding and molecules are included and coupled to the dust physics, pre-supernova stellar feedback is included, and the sampling of supernova and AGN feedback is improved. In addition, besides the fiducial model that uses thermally-driven AGN feedback, we include simulations that employ hybrid thermally-driven/kinetic jet-driven AGN feedback, where the hybrid model tracks the BH spin and predicts radio emission from relativistic electrons.

We view COLIBRE as a stepping stone between very high-resolution simulations of ISM patches of isolated galaxies, and the previous generation of simulations of representative volumes. Although COLIBRE cannot resolve individual molecular clouds, it captures key effects of a cold interstellar phase that has a small volume filling factor while comprising a large fraction of the ISM mass. Including a cold phase also opens up an entirely new set of questions, such as the evolution and distribution of atomic and molecular hydrogen, and the effect of dust on chemistry and observables on scales exceeding molecular cloud sizes. Although simulations that pressurize the ISM and do not explicitly model the evolution of dust grains have been used to address these questions, their analysis requires strong assumptions and relies on post-processing gas and dust distributions that are unlikely to be sufficiently realistic.

Simulation Sizes

The COLIBRE suite uses a wedding cake design, with larger volumes using a progressively lower resolution. As we find the model to converge better than EAGLE, we use a wider range of resolutions. The flagship simulations each use 5x30083 particles in cubic volumes of 200 and 400 cMpc on a side, yielding a particle mass for both baryons and DM of ~106 and ~107Msun, respectively. We also present a higher-resolution model with particle mass ~107Msun in a 25 cMpc box, and which is still running in a 50 cMpc box. We will refer to the high, intermediate, and low COLIBRE resolutions as m5, m6, and m7, respectively. A list of the simulations in the suite can be found here.
