The COLIBRE Project

COLIBRE is a project of the Virgo consortium for cosmological supercomputer simulations. The acronym stands for COLd Ism and Better REsolution, where the ‘better resolution’ refers to the larger ratio of DM to baryonic particles compared with most previous simulations, which increases the effective resolution for a fixed baryonic particle mass and gravitational softening length.

Key Features

  • Cold (T << 104 K) interstellar gas
  • New cooling (non-equil. H, He, e-; self-shielding, UVB and interstellar radiation)
  • Dust (3 grain species, 2 grain sizes; coupled to cooling)
  • New ‘chemistry’ (new yields, diffusion, new SNIa, s- and r-process elements)
  • New stellar feedback (HII regions, winds, turbulence, improved stochastic SN feedback)
  • Improved BH and AGN physics (also runs with BH spin and jet feedback)
  • New star formation (Schmidt law with instability criterion)
  • Model for globular clusters (m5 only)

As was the case for EAGLE, the subgrid feedback is calibrated to reproduce the observed z=0 galaxy stellar mass function and galaxy half-mass radii, and the AGN feedback efficiency is calibrated to the BH masses of massive z=0 galaxies. While this calibration was done by hand for EAGLE, COLIBRE uses machine learning to perform the calibration to the mass function and sizes at our lowest resolution. We then make small adjustments by hand when we increase the resolution or switch from thermal to hybrid AGN feedback.
